# Decisions

Key choices and why we made them: the "why" only. What changed is in [progress.md](progress.md); the resulting design is in [architecture.md](architecture.md). Newest at the bottom. Each entry has: date · decision · why · rejected · decided by.

### D-001 · 2026-11-12 · A fictional used-car retailer's support KB
- **Why:** it comes from a real experience: a retailer's chatbot gave a wrong answer that depended on policies linked to each other. Support KBs are a natural RAG use case, and a synthetic KB can be read end to end and checked by eye.
- **Rejected:** a slice of a public multi-hop dataset (too big to read, harder entity extraction); reusing an earlier Sherlock Holmes corpus (769 chunks, impossible to verify by eye).
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-002 · 2026-11-12 · Use the Claude API for the LLM steps
- **Why:** graph extraction and answer generation should be real model calls you can watch (prompt in, JSON out, written to the graph). Model: `claude-opus-5-5`.
- **Rejected:** a retrieval-only version with a hand-written extraction.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-003 · 2026-11-12 · The audience is developers learning ArangoDB, and the repo is all English
- **Why:** Tianyi: "the cookbook is like the recipe (for developers to learn the tools/product Arango is offering)". It has to be shareable.
- **Rejected:** v0's personal learning lab with Chinese explanation cards.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-004 · 2026-11-12 · Fictional states
- **Why:** state rules are what make conditional questions interesting, but a public cookbook must not assert real law.
- **Rejected:** real states with a disclaimer (readers may take it as fact); no states (loses the conditional questions).
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-005 · 2026-11-12 · Two graph layers: structural (parsed) and semantic (LLM)
- **Why:** a hand-written facts graph puts the answers into the graph, and an evaluation on it proves nothing. That's the circular-evaluation mistake from an earlier project. The structural layer comes from the KB's own links, form codes, versions and states, which a real KB has too. The semantic layer is the usual GraphRAG extraction, and it's scored against a hand-labelled sample.
- **Rejected:** structural only (not what people mean by GraphRAG); LLM only (loses "your KB is already a graph", and versions would be guessed).
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-006 · 2026-11-12 · Gold articles are decided from the answer, then frozen
- **Why:** a question's gold articles are the ones a correct answer must cite. They are decided when the question is written, never from the graph or from any method's output, and frozen (commit + sha256) before any method runs. That keeps the evaluation honest.
- **Decided by:** Claude, agreed in the plan

### D-007 · 2026-11-12 · Form codes get their own field with the `identity` analyzer
- **Why:** `text_en` splits `NW-POA-2` into `nw`, `poa`, `2`, so BM25 can hardly tell it from `NW-POA-1` (I-002). Exact identifiers need exact matching.
- **Decided by:** Claude, from the environment check

### D-008 · 2026-11-12 · "Today" is fixed at 2026-11-01
- **Why:** temporal questions must give the same result whatever day the notebook runs.
- **Decided by:** Claude

### D-009 · 2026-11-12 · Medium size
- **What:** 51 articles / 68 versions, ~200 tickets, 15 forms, 5 states, 12 lenders, 6 lessors, 25 stores; ~56 gold questions.
- **Why:** 30 versions were too few for the methods to differ; this is enough to play with and still small enough to review.
- **Rejected:** articles only (~90 versions, no tickets or stores); large (~120 versions, 500 tickets, ~80 questions: too long to write and review).
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-010 · 2026-11-12 · Tickets are generated by a script that calls Claude
- **Why:** varied, natural customer language for semantic search, and the script itself is a reusable recipe. Python decides the facts (date, cited version, outdated or not, agent mistake or not), so the generated text can't drift from the plan.
- **Rejected:** Claude Code writing them by hand (slow, one voice); templates (repetitive, useless for semantic search).
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-011 · 2026-11-12 · All four kinds of tricky cases
- **What:** time boundaries, rule conflicts and combinations, messy input, messy KB.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-017 · 2026-11-13 · Recall@10 is the headline; BM25 is the baseline
- **Why:** Tianyi's evaluation plan: run BM25 and vector search on the gold set and compare them at Recall@10. The other metrics (Recall@5, All-gold@k, MRR, trap@k, stale@k) are reported alongside.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-018 · 2026-11-13 · Stop when a score drops
- **What:** at the end of every stage, run the checks in `eval.md`. If a score drops and no change was meant to cause it, stop and wait for Tianyi.
- **Why:** if the agent adjusts things on its own, a drop must never slip through.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-019 · 2026-11-13 · Two dates per question: `asked_on` and `as_of`
- **Why:** some questions ask about the past or the future, so the rules that apply aren't always today's. The gold articles and stale@k use `as_of`. Filtering by `asked_on` is the naive approach, and it fails on purpose for the historical questions.
- **Decided by:** Claude, while drafting the gold set (to be confirmed by Tianyi in his review)

### D-020 · 2026-11-13 · Answer facts are graded by meaning
- **Why:** `answer_facts` are short paraphrases or computed values, and an exact string match would mark correct answers wrong. A script check over the 40 answerable questions found 24 facts that aren't verbatim in their gold articles, none of them contradictions.
- **Decided by:** Claude (stage 4 implements the judge)

### D-021 · 2026-11-13 · Quality gates before the tickets count as done
- **What:** generated tickets must pass the faithfulness and variety checks (thresholds set before checking). Failing tickets are regenerated by fixing the plan or the prompt, never by hand-editing the text or loosening a threshold after seeing the results.
- **Why:** the tickets aren't gold, but they're part of the corpus every method searches, so duplicates or invented facts would distort the evaluation.
- **Cost:** the first batch ($1.46) was replaced. Total spent on tickets is about $3.10 (batch 1 $1.46, batch 2 $1.57, 4 regenerations and 2 single tests about $0.07). The dry-run estimate ($2.68 per batch) was too high, because output was ~230 tokens per ticket, not 600.
- **Decided by:** Claude, inside the budget Tianyi chose ($1–3), ending slightly above it

### D-022 · 2026-11-13 · Vague questions: grade what the message says; clarify later
- **What:** vague questions join the gold set in three cases: missing term, ambiguous, and wrong term (eval.md). Ambiguous ones list every plausible article as gold, and any one counts. When the customer uses the wrong term, the message's literal meaning is the ground truth. Clarifying follow-ups ("Did you mean…?") are planned for stage 5, not built now.
- **Why:** real traffic is vague; a gold set of precise questions overstates every method. But the system can't be blamed for answering what was literally asked, so wrong-term messages are graded on their words.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi

### D-023 · 2026-11-17 · Real state and city names; rules stay synthetic (reverses D-004)
- **What:** Aldora → Virginia, Brevin → North Carolina, Corlan → Georgia, Dunmore → Texas, Eastvale → California; 25 store cities replaced by real ones with approximate coordinates. Rules, policies, companies and numbers are unchanged. Done by `scripts/rename_places.py` before the gold freeze and before any embeddings, so nothing had to be re-generated ($0). The archived ticket batch keeps the old names as evidence.
- **Why:** Tianyi wants the demo to read like a real retailer's KB. Real names make the conditional questions recognisable; a disclaimer at the top of the README and `data/kb/README.md` says the rules are synthetic.
- **Risk accepted:** a reader may take a synthetic rule as real law. Mitigated by the disclaimer; no rule was changed to look more realistic.
- **Rejected:** keeping fictional states (D-004); renaming only the cities (gives "Austin, Aldora").
- **Decided by:** Tianyi (2026-11-17)

### D-024 · 2026-11-17 · The gold set is 30 questions, chosen from the questions alone
- **What:** 16 seeds Tianyi kept + 14 drafts, in the mix multi_hop 7, checklist 5, temporal 5, conditional 4, exact 3, semantic 3, unanswerable 3. Each added draft has a written reason (`why_in_30`). The other 26 go to `data/eval/gold_reserve.json` unchanged; retrieval code is debugged on the reserve, never on the 30. Frozen after Tianyi's review (commit + sha256 in `data/eval/FROZEN`).
- **Why:** Tianyi can review 30 in about 25 minutes, not 56. The mix leans to the types where the KB's structure should matter (multi_hop, checklist, temporal), because that is the claim being tested; the README says so. No method existed when the 30 were picked, so the choice can't be fitted to results.
- **Rejected:** equal counts per type (fewer questions that test the graph); adding messy/vague candidates now (longer review; they come later).
- **Decided by:** Tianyi (approach); Claude (the 14 picks, pending Tianyi's review)

### D-025 · 2026-11-17 · Four retrieval methods, one change each; the graph rule is fixed before any run
- **What:** M1 BM25 over all versions · M2 vector over all versions · M3 = M2 + filter (in effect on `as_of`, applies to the customer's state) · M4 = M3 + graph expansion. M4's rule: candidates are M3's top 10; the top 5 are entry points; from each entry, walk `kb_graph` to other articles over `links_to` (either direction, 1 hop) or through one shared form, lender or lessor (article → form/lender/lessor ← article, 2 hops; `uses_form` → `replaced_by` → `uses_form` is 3 hops). Never through another article, a state (`applies_in` connects everything in a state) or `supersedes`. Neighbors must pass M3's filter. A candidate's score = max(its own cosine similarity, parent score × 0.8^hops). Sort, take top k. The graph can add or raise an article, never lower one.
- **Why:** each step adds one thing, so the table shows what the filter adds and what the graph adds on top (an ablation). 0.8 and "top 5 entries" are picked a priori, not tuned; any later sensitivity table will be reported as such, without switching to the best value.
- **Rejected:** three methods (can't tell the filter's gain from the graph's); hybrid RRF as a fifth (later); LLM-extracted graph (later, stage 4).
- **Decided by:** Tianyi (ladder); Claude (the expansion rule)

### D-026 · 2026-11-17 · Recall@5 is the headline; no precision (replaces D-017's Recall@10)
- **What:** per question, over the top-k article versions: Recall@5 (headline), Recall@10, All-gold@5, MRR@10, nDCG@5 (binary relevance), stale@5 (a version not in effect on `as_of`), trap@5. Averaged by type and for a multi-hop + checklist slice; each cell says how many questions it covers. The 3 unanswerable questions are left out of retrieval metrics (eval.md) and scored in the answer eval. A per-question win/loss list (M4 vs M3) is part of the output.
- **Why:** the KB has 50 current articles, so K=10 is a fifth of it and hides differences. Questions have 1.5 gold articles on average, so Precision@10 can't go above about 0.15: it says nothing here.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi (approved with the plan, 2026-11-17)

### D-027 · 2026-11-17 · Freshness demo: change how customers get paid, in a copy of the database
- **What:** `make fresh` rebuilds `northwind_kb_demo`, asks "How will you pay me for my car, and how long does it take?", then in one stream transaction inserts `getting_paid__2026-11` (instant transfer instead of 1 to 3 business days), closes `getting_paid__2026` on 2026-10-31, adds a `supersedes` edge, parses the new version's own edges and points existing `links_to` edges at it. It asks again right after the commit and walks `supersedes` to show the history. Transcript: `data/eval/fresh_demo.md`.
- **Why this policy:** payment timing is a policy a retailer really changes, its article already has a 2022 → 2026 history, and no gold question is touched (the demo runs in a copy).
- **Why this question:** it matches the article's own subject. The first wording tried ("how fast does the money get to my bank account?") didn't bring the article into any vector method's top 3, so the demo couldn't show the switch. That attempt is logged in progress.md; the question was changed once, not searched for.
- **What it shows, honestly:** the switch to the new version comes from the date filter plus versioning (M3); M4's graph expansion adds nothing in this case. The graph's part is `supersedes` (the history) and the re-linking. Plain vector search (M2) returns the 2022 version first, before and after the change.
- **Decided by:** Tianyi (live policy change); Claude (policy and question)
